Meet Granola

A notepad that listens

You type a few fragments. When the call ends, Granola writes the notes you would have written if you had an hour to spare.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola

Granola is a desktop app for Mac and Windows, now also on iPhone, Android and Apple Watch. It reads your calendar, so it knows when a meeting starts. It listens to the audio on your own computer instead of sending a recording bot into the call, so it works the same on Zoom, Meet, Teams, a phone call or a conversation across a table.1

1
Before the meeting
A short brief: who is attending, and what you discussed last time.
2
During it
You jot only what matters to you: “pricing objection”, “Sarah owns Q3”.
3
When it ends
Your fragments and the transcript become structured notes, action items and a draft follow-up email.
4
Weeks later
You can question every meeting at once: “What did I commit to this week?”

One misconception to drop early: this is not a transcription app. Otter, the category’s older leader, sends a visible bot and gives you something like a court stenographer’s record. Granola tries to give you the one-page memo a sharp chief of staff would write. To Granola, the transcript is raw material. The note is the product.

1 hr+

The time heavy users report saving each day at four to five meetings.2 Below about three meetings a day, the product is nice to have rather than essential. Judgment

The product

Your notes are the skeleton

Two early decisions define Granola: your own typed lines anchor the AI’s write-up, and the audio is never kept.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola
Simplified from Granola’s help docs and founder interviews, as summarised in the research notes (Oct 16, 2026). Which model does which job is not disclosed.

The hard part of summarising a meeting is not shortening it. It is knowing what mattered. The few words you type are that signal. Granola turns a hard judgement problem into an easier expansion problem: the write-up is built around your lines, and the detail comes from the transcript, with a link back to the passage it came from.1

The second decision was about trust. Granola transcribes as you go and throws the audio away. The trade-off is real: there is no recording to replay, and on desktop the speaker labels are mostly just “Me” and “Them”, the gap rivals cite most.2

“

When we first created Granola, we made a very conscious decision not to record and store any audio … And everyone kind of laughed at us for that.

Chris Pedregal, CEO · Invest Like the Best, Feb 25, 2025

2Product gaps from critical reviews and a competitor’s comparison page cited in the research (Sep–Oct 2026); S1, S24.

The customer

Built for the busiest person in the room

Granola started with investors and founders: the people with the most meetings and the loudest networks. Often, the champion is also the boss.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola

In the 2024 cohort, 57% of users held leadership roles.1 When the champion is the boss, adoption moves down through the company, and it moves quickly.

Origin

A year of silence, then growth by word of mouth

The two founders had never worked together. They held off launching for nine months against investor advice, then grew with almost no marketing.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola

The founders say the delay was deliberate. “Typical VC actually been pushing us to launch for about nine months before that. We held them off for nine months,” Stephenson said in May 2025.1 The rival Pedregal chose to worry about was “the one that hasn’t launched yet”.2

The growth came from people sharing it. “Most of our virality is … either just people recommending it to each other or people sharing notes with each other,” Stephenson said in April 2026. Every shared note is an invitation. That loop has to offset a cost he names himself: without a bot, there is no “huge glowing orb with a logo on it” in other people’s meetings.3

“

We were seven years late when we founded Granola. We didn’t launch for a year.

Chris Pedregal · Invest Like the Best, Feb 25, 2025

Startup Teardown · Granola05 / 14
Positioning

The identity held. The ambition moved twice.

In 26 months Granola’s stated scope widened, then narrowed. The open question was who does the work with the context.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola
Author’s reading of dated statements: launch post, Invest Like the Best, Generative Now, MAD, AI & I. Height is qualitative. Judgment

Through 2025 the ambition widened. In May, Pedregal said “meetings are really just to start like we’ll have to add emails, we have to add Slack”. By August, Granola was “a tool for thought to help you do work”.1 In early 2026 the record opened: an MCP server,2 then, after an a16z partner publicly quit over newly encrypted local files on March 16, a public API within the week.3 By July the plan was narrower: do meeting-adjacent jobs, and feed context to whatever agent the customer uses.

The seam still shows. In June the VP of Sales said “the product will start using the context of your conversations to help you get work done.”4 Email and Slack as inputs, first floated in May 2025, had not visibly shipped by October 2026. Gap

“

Be best in the world in anything that’s like meeting adjacent and then … be the best way to capture context to power any agents you have.

Chris Pedregal · AI & I (Every), Jul 15, 2026, 39:56

The category

The standalone race is mostly decided

Among businesses paying for a dedicated AI notetaker, Granola was the default by autumn 2026.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola
Ramp vendor data, updated Oct 2, 2026. Buyers can pay more than one vendor; Ramp’s customer base skews toward small and mid-sized firms; Otter is not shown in Ramp’s category view. Reported

Two independent spending datasets point the same way. On Ramp’s card data, 64% of businesses buying AI meeting notes were paying Granola, up 34 points in a year, and 74% of its buyers there had switched from a competitor.1 In YipitData’s mid-market panel, churn was near zero, spend tripled in six months, and switching ran mostly one way: toward Granola.2

One real advantage, and only against some rivals

Counter-positioning means a rival cannot copy you without hurting itself. Otter’s growth depends on its visible bot, which advertises the product in every call it joins. Dropping it would break that loop. Google’s value is the surface it owns, so it cannot be neutral across Zoom and Teams. Through 2026, both moved further into their own products instead. That explains the switching data better than any feature.

But it only works against incumbents whose models punish imitation. Notion, ChatGPT Record and Anarlog are all bot-free. Against them, Granola’s idea is a well-made feature with a strong brand, not a structural power. Judgment

The business

A notes-app price, with costs that grow with use

Revenue is undisclosed. What is visible: a price ladder built to recruit teams, and costs that rise with the most-loved features.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola
Per user per month. History reconstructed from press because archived pages were unreachable; current tiers from granola.ai/pricing, Oct 2, 2026. Reported

The company has said annual recurring revenue grew more than 400% in the year to March 2026, and 2.5 times between January and the Series C announcement.1 That jump coincided with removing the individual plan, so it is not a steady rate. Sales works bottom-up: reps take adoption that already exists in an account and “formalize it in a company contract.”2 On Oct 2, 2026, eight of 18 open roles were in go-to-market.3

Costs are less settled. “There was a time where half of our burn rate as a company was going on transcription,” Stephenson said.4 That is now cheap; the new cost is cross-meeting AI work: about half of weekly users run agentic queries, and Granola prepares meeting briefs even though only about 10% are opened.5

“

Agentic features are really expensive um and so as we build more and more of them at some point that’ll … break the math.

Chris Pedregal · AI & I, Jul 15, 2026, 56:45

Gross margin is probably 50–70%, nearer the low end, below typical software. The CEO said only that it was not negative (Aug 2025). Estimate

The model question

Better models help Granola. They help everyone else too.

The usual case against an app built on someone else’s AI is that the next model release makes it pointless. In Granola’s case that is the wrong test.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola

Granola does not train a frontier model and does not try to. “Our philosophy is to use the best model that is on the market as quickly as … possible,” Pedregal said in 2025.1 What it owns is the workflow around the model: when capture starts, what the user typed, which past meetings are relevant, and where the result goes next. So far, three things have meant each model generation helped Granola more than it hurt.

1
Longer context opened up the product
At launch “you could only do like 30 minute meetings.” Larger context windows removed that limit and, later, made searching across many meetings practical.2
2
The hard questions need the whole record
Simple retrieval finds facts. But for questions like “what did I explain badly?”, a lot of the most interesting queries “would completely fail with that type of method.” Only the full context gives a good answer.3
3
It builds a year ahead
The company aims to “build for the world a year from now,” on the bet that capabilities which are expensive today will be cheap by the time they reach users.3

The catch is that the same models are available to Google, OpenAI and the open-source copies. The real test is relative: does Granola improve faster than the free version? Stephenson has said he has “worried about this since day one,” and, when asked why users still pay, “I don’t have great answers as to why.”4 Judgment

“

If Granola doesn’t get better when … the next version of Claude or GPT comes out, then I think over … time we’re cooked.

Sam Stephenson, co-founder · Cognitive Revolution, Apr 8, 2026, 1:30:33

The risks

The lawsuit targets the core feature

Capturing a call without a visible bot is what users love. It is also what a pending class action says is illegal.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola

Chamberlain v. Granola was filed in federal court in Northern California on July 30, 2026. It alleges that recording other people in a meeting without their consent, and training models on those recordings, breaks California’s wiretap law and federal law. These are allegations, not findings.1 Two weeks later, the same court refused to dismiss similar claims against Otter. It held that a notetaker that trains on recordings for its own purposes can be treated as a third-party eavesdropper.2

Granola’s defence was in place before the suit was filed: “granola from the get-go never stored the audio. It only stores the transcripts.”3 But reporting in April and July 2026 described defaults that cut the other way: share links anyone could open, training switched on by default for non-enterprise users, and folders shared across a whole organisation.4 As of Oct 2, 2026, coverage showed no answer or motion to dismiss; the case may have moved since. Startup Teardown · Granola12 / 14
The verdict

Running the race well, without controlling the clock

Granola has won the paid notetaker market. The $1.5B price depends on a second act that it does not yet own.

Startup Teardown · No.01 · Granola
The optimist’s case

The best retention in the category and the strongest switching data. A neutral record of every meeting, available to any agent through MCP and an API. A founding team that delayed launch, kept a clear identity, and cuts features to keep the product calm.

The sceptic’s case

A small category whose price is heading towards zero. Every repeatable move has been copied. Margins that agent features will squeeze. A lawsuit aimed at the defining feature. No disclosed revenue, users or renewals.

Our reading: both are right, on different timescales. The first act was won on product quality, not on a structural moat. The second act, supplying context to agents, is the right direction, but the agents, buyers and budgets are not yet in place. In October 2026 the research put the window for turning brand and meeting history into organisation-level lock-in at about 9 to 18 months, before free, automatic notes become the default box ticked for small teams. Judgment
