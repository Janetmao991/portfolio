The writing framework

2 pages

The research runs long. What I publish fits on two pages, always in this order.

Page 1Why it wins

  1. HeadlineThe company in one plain image, then one sentence: what it won, what the price is for.

  2. Key numbersFour or five figures: valuation, money raised, share, revenue, headcount. Each dated.

  3. LedeWhat it is, in plain words, ending on the bet.

  4. 01

    How it startedThe founders and the first move that worked.

  5. 02

    Advantage and moatThe edge against each kind of rival, and what is not a moat.

  6. 03

    Where it standsWhat it has already won, and where that market tops out.

Two-pager1 / 2
Page 2What the price pays for

  1. The verdict, up frontThe call and its time window, before any argument.

  2. 04

    The ambitionThe bigger bet, and how the founders' story of it has changed by date.

  3. 05

    The economicsMargins and costs: who is profitable, and what will change the pricing.

  4. 06

    The biggest riskThe one risk that would end the bet, with odds.

  5. VerdictThe call, the time window, and three signals to watch.

Two-pager2 / 2

Rules

  1. Two pages, six sections. Why it wins, then what the price pays for. Nothing else.
  2. Headings are claims. Each section title states its conclusion, not its topic.
  3. Lead with the point. Every paragraph opens with what it means; evidence follows.
  4. Quotes with timestamps. Each founder quote links to the second it was said, and gets one line on what it implies.
  5. Estimates say so. Anything undisclosed is a range marked as my estimate.
  6. End on what would change it. Odds on the biggest risk, a time window, three signals to watch.
